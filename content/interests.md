# Interests

My central interest is theoretical mathematics. Machine learning theory is an important secondary direction, but the core of my intellectual program is mathematical: algebraic topology, finite group theory, equivariant homotopy theory, formal proof, and the long-range structures surrounding the Langlands program.

## Algebraic Topology

Algebraic topology is currently my main mathematical direction. I am interested in how homotopical invariants, fixed point constructions, and categorical structures encode global information. In particular, I am drawn to problems where a geometric or homotopical object can be reconstructed from local, fixed point, or subgroup-level data.

Current themes include:

- equivariant homotopy theory and fixed point methods;
- geometric fixed point tomography;
- Bökstedt periodicity and related periodic phenomena;
- stable homotopy-theoretic structures;
- homological and categorical methods in topology;
- interactions between topology, representation theory, and algebraic geometry.

## Finite Group Theory and Quillen-Type Problems

I am interested in finite groups through the topology of subgroup complexes and local group-theoretic structure. My current work on Quillen's p-subgroup conjecture is motivated by the possibility that centralizer geometry and sphere-like structures can expose hidden constraints in p-local group theory.

Topics include:

- Quillen's p-subgroup conjecture;
- centralizer energy;
- FRUL-WOS spheres;
- p-subgroup complexes;
- centralizer and normalizer structures;
- topological methods in finite group theory.

## Langlands Program

The Langlands program is a long-range interest rather than a short-term claim of specialization. I am especially interested in its geometric and representation-theoretic aspects: the way algebraic geometry, automorphic forms, Galois representations, representation theory, sheaf theory, and categorical structures meet in a unified mathematical architecture.

My current plan is foundational and cumulative: to build the algebraic geometry, representation theory, homological algebra, and derived/categorical language required to eventually work seriously around the geometric Langlands program and its topological shadows.

## Formal Proof and Lean

I view formal proof as part of the future infrastructure of mathematics. My interest in Lean is not merely computational; it is mathematical. I care about how formalization changes the way definitions are designed, how proof dependencies are organized, and how large theories in algebra, topology, and analysis can be made mechanically checkable.

I participate in the Zhejiang University Lean Formalization Summer School, focusing on Lean, Mathlib, proof engineering, and the formalization of advanced mathematical arguments.

## Foundational Machine Learning Theory

My machine learning theory interests are mathematical rather than engineering-oriented. I am interested in the formal structure of learning algorithms, especially where probability, geometry, optimization, and dynamics are unavoidable.

Topics include:

- statistical learning theory;
- generalization and learnability;
- out-of-distribution prediction;
- optimization-theoretic foundations of learning;
- dynamical-systems views of deep and autoregressive models;
- mathematical foundations of representation learning.
